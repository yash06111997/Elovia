## 2. Teardowns by mechanism (what's actually doing the work)

These follow the viral reverse-engineering method: tear down each layer, then run the
counterfactual ("remove this; does it still work?") to separate the **drivers** from the noise.

### 2a. "AI food yelling at you"

Example: an onion, angry about being refrigerated. A rice bowl yells about reheating.

| Layer | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–1s) | An extreme close-up of a food with a face, already shouting **at you** ("STOP putting me in the fridge") | **DRIVER** |
| Emotion | Indignation turned into comedy, and being *told off* by your dinner | **DRIVER** |
| Retention | One tip, 10–30s, a single clear payoff | Supporting |
| Format | Lip-synced AI voice, burned-in captions, no CTA | Incidental (copyable by anyone) |
| Angle | "You've been doing it wrong" | Supporting |
| Share trigger | Practical value plus "this is SO my mum" (tag) | **DRIVER** |
| Distribution | Novelty wave, Dec–Jan | **CONFOUND.** It won't stay novel |

**Mechanism:** a food character confronts *you* about a habit, and you come away with something
useful. Advice from a cartoon feels less like being judged than advice from a person [A/B: media
psychologist quoted by Dexerto; The Tab].
**Replicability:** the mechanism transfers; the novelty doesn't. Thousands of clones by late
January [A], so a lone "yelling food" clip is now generic.
**Elovia's version:** Elo plus the Food Gang. Food is the *antagonist*, not the teacher, and the
**scan card is the payoff**. Anyone can make a food yell. Only Elovia can resolve the scene with
its own app.

### 2b. Serialised character drama (Fruit Love Island)

| Layer | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | A reality-TV parody setup, recognisable in a second | Supporting |
| Emotion | Gossip, romance, betrayal | **DRIVER** |
| Retention | **Numbered episodes with cliffhangers.** Viewers come back for the next one | **DRIVER** |
| Format | Named recurring characters with one trait each | **DRIVER** (it's what builds a fandom) |
| Share trigger | Fandom: "did you see episode 9?!" | **DRIVER** |
| Distribution | First mover in March 2026 | CONFOUND |

**Mechanism:** recurring characters plus unresolved stakes produce follows, not just views.
**Watch out:** TikTok removed many episodes and the creator stopped after about 22 [A]. **Don't
parody trademarked shows or formats.** Elovia's arcs (Wedding Season Part 1, 2, 3; the Food Gang
finale) borrow the episode structure, not the IP.

### 2c. Unhinged mascot (Duolingo)

| Layer | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Character | One exaggerated trait (passive-aggressive), always the same | **DRIVER** |
| Product tie | The joke *is* the product behaviour (streak guilt) | **DRIVER** |
| Format | Reacts to culture, rarely sells directly | Supporting |
| Distribution | Years of consistency, run by a human social lead | **CONFOUND.** Expect it to take months |

**Mechanism:** a mascot whose personality is a product behaviour. Elo's single trait: *honest
about the number*. The product behaviour it maps to: **scanning**.

### 2d. Hyper-real AI humans (why we're *not* doing this)

They grab attention quickly ("is she AI?" debates), then:
- Engagement collapses once people realise. Jean Phil's engagement halved within about four weeks [A].
- Platforms remove or limit them. Since **31 Aug 2026**, Instagram limits recommendation reach for AI-persona accounts that don't carry the **"AI-generated profile"** label [A: TechCrunch, Engadget].
- They're legally exposed. AI testimonials are banned under the **US FTC fake-reviews rule** (about $53K per violation) [A]. In India, **ASCI** says virtual influencers must disclose they aren't human [A], and the **amended IT Rules (in force 20 Feb 2026)** require labels on synthetic content that looks real [A].
- They lose money on YouTube. **Since July 2026**, YouTube treats AI personas posing as health experts as unmonetisable [A].
- **Audiences are tired of AI slop.** Gartner (Sep 2026): 65% of consumers say brands make too much AI content and 57% trust brand messaging less [A]. In Sprout Social's Q1 2026 survey, 50% of Gen Z had unfollowed or blocked a brand over AI slop [A].

If the account you saw uses a realistic AI human, **this is the biggest way to beat them**: a
stylised, openly-AI character doesn't have a "reveal" to lose engagement over.

### 2e. Real creators scanning plates (Cal AI)

| Layer | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | A messy real plate, and the scan result **within the first seconds** [B] | **DRIVER** |
| Proof | A real person, a real phone, a real number | **DRIVER** (this is what converts) |
| Distribution | About 20 new creators a week at peak, paid against reach targets [A] | Volume, the CONFOUND that money buys |

**Mechanism:** the product demo *is* the hook. Elovia adopts the **number reveal early** and a
**real screen recording** in every video. In phase 2, add real creators (see `06`).

## 3. India-specific signals

- **Hinglish beats polished English** for Reels in fitness and food. Keep it under 30 seconds with
  the payoff early [B: multiple India-focused guides]. Meta reported in June 2026 that fitness is
  among India's most-engaged Reels genres [A].
- **Relatable desi framing wins** in Hindi fitness Shorts: "maa ka diet" at 12.7M views, a
  "budget-friendly diet" Short at 42.2M [B: Modash listings]. Low budget, family context, recognition.
- **Label-reading and food-truth content works**: FoodPharmer (about 4M IG followers) built
  "Label Padhega India" on humour plus exposés [B]. The format transfers to Elo; the targeting of
  brands doesn't (stay generic, no brand call-outs).
- **Timely angles:** wedding season (Nov–Feb), leftover Diwali mithai, vegetarian protein (very
  hot, with brands and Bollywood pushing protein [A: Reuters]), the samosa/jalebi "warning board"
  rumour from July 2025 that PIB debunked [A].
- **Avoid:** GLP-1 / weight-loss injections. "Mounjaro brides" is in the news, but CDSCO's Mar 2026
  advisory warns against direct or indirect promotion, including by influencers [A]. Also avoid
  "AI doctor" content; deepfaked doctors led to FIRs and court action [A].
- **No named Indian talking-roti, talking-samosa or talking-organ account with verifiable reach
  was found** [B]. The space looks open.

## 4. How to beat the avatar you saw: 8 levers

Whatever their character is, these are the gaps viral AI-character accounts usually leave:

1. **Make the product the plot.** Most AI-character accounts get views and no installs because the
   product isn't in the story. Elo's scan card *is* the app.
2. **One owned host plus a rotating cast.** Viewers follow a *character*, not a format. A rotating
   cast of foods alone builds nothing that lasts.
3. **Real numbers from a real database.** Their food yells; Elo gives you a number you can use.
   Usefulness drives saves.
4. **Openly AI and stylised.** No reveal, so nothing collapses. Label it, and still be good.
5. **Series with cliffhangers** (Part 1, 2, 3) and recurring villains, so viewers follow instead of watching once.
6. **The comment loop.** "Comment your food, Elo scans it next" turns the audience into the writers' room.
7. **Localisation as a moat.** One production, two voice tracks (Hinglish and English). The Indian
   food database (71 dishes) is something US competitors don't have.
8. **A real human layer for trust.** Founder behind-the-scenes plus real creators in phase 2, the
   way Cal AI turned attention into installs.

copy of someone else's character is both a legal risk and a strategic dead end.
